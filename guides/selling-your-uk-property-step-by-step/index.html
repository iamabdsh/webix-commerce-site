Selling Your UK Property: A Step-by-Step Guide

Selling a property involves more than putting up a listing and waiting. There is a sequence of steps — valuation, preparation, viewings, offers, legal work and completion — and knowing the order helps you plan timescales and avoid surprises. This guide walks through the main stages of selling a property in the UK.

1. Get a realistic valuation

Start with a realistic idea of what your property is worth. Look at recent sale prices for similar properties locally and consider getting valuations from more than one source. Pricing realistically from the start usually attracts more interest than starting high and reducing later.

2. Prepare the property

Before viewings, deal with the basics: repair obvious faults, tidy and declutter, and make sure the property photographs well. First impressions shape how buyers judge value, and most buyers see the photographs before they see the property.

3. Choose how to sell

Most sellers use an estate agent, but it is not the only route. Some sell privately, and some sell directly to an investor or a property company — which can suit sellers who value a simpler process or a quicker timescale over achieving the very top price. Think about which matters most for your situation before you commit.

4. Viewings and offers

During viewings, buyers are comparing your property with everything else they have seen. When offers come in, the highest offer is not always the strongest: a buyer’s position — whether they are chain-free, have a mortgage agreed in principle, or still need to sell their own property — affects how likely the sale is to complete.

5. Conveyancing and completion

Once an offer is accepted, solicitors or conveyancers handle the legal transfer: contracts, searches and enquiries, then exchange of contracts and completion. This stage takes time and depends on everyone in the chain, so regular updates and quick replies to your solicitor help keep things moving.
