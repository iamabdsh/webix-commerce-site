Shopify, TikTok Shop or eBay: Which Platform Should UK Sellers Start With?

Choosing where to sell is one of the first decisions a UK online seller makes, and there is no single right answer. Shopify, TikTok Shop and eBay work in very different ways: one gives you your own store, one sells through social content, and one puts your products in front of an existing marketplace audience. This guide explains how each platform works and what it asks of you, so you can choose the right place to start.

Shopify: your own online store

With Shopify you sell through your own online store, under your own brand. You control how the store looks, how products are presented and how customers check out. That control is the main advantage — your store is an asset your business owns, rather than a page inside someone else’s marketplace.

The trade-off is traffic. A new Shopify store starts with no audience, so customers have to reach you through search, social media, advertising or repeat business. Shopify suits sellers who want to build a long-term brand and are ready to work on bringing visitors in. The ongoing work includes store setup, product listings, optimisation and day-to-day account management.

TikTok Shop: selling where people scroll

TikTok Shop lets customers buy products they discover in videos, livestreams and the app’s shopping areas. Instead of waiting for shoppers to search, your products are shown to people while they browse — which can suit visual products that are easy to demonstrate.

The catch is that content is part of the job. TikTok Shop works best when products are shown regularly in short videos or live sessions, and when listings are kept optimised for how people browse the app. Sellers who enjoy creating content, or who can work with creators, usually get the most from it.

eBay: an established marketplace

eBay is a long-established marketplace where buyers come already looking to purchase. Your listings sit alongside other sellers’, and buyers can compare price, condition and delivery at a glance. Listings can be fixed price or auction, and the marketplace handles a large part of the trust side of the transaction.

That makes eBay a practical starting point for many sellers, including those selling unique, refurbished or one-off items that are hard to build a standalone store around. The ongoing work is account handling, listing creation and optimisation, and keeping on top of messages and orders.

How to choose where to start

Three questions usually make the decision clearer. First, where are your customers — are they searching on a marketplace, scrolling social media, or likely to come to your own store? Second, what are you selling — branded products you want to build a name around, items that demonstrate well on video, or stock that suits a marketplace? Third, how much time can you give the channel each week — every platform rewards consistent listings, optimisation and customer care.

Many UK sellers end up using more than one platform over time, but most are better off starting with one and running it properly before adding another.

Getting help with the day-to-day work

Whichever platform you choose, the management work is similar: setting up the store or account, creating and optimising listings, and keeping everything running.
